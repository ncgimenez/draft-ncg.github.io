SNCB: Know Which Way to Run Before You Get There

A connecting passenger doesn't know which platform they're arriving on, or which side of the train to exit from. The data exists — it's just not shown at the right moment.

Personal concept, not affiliated with SNCB or Infrabel. This concept came from a concrete experience: arriving at Brussels-Midi with a tight connection window, not knowing which platform my train would arrive on, and having to scan departure boards from the wrong end of the station before running to the right one. Speaking informally with regular commuters, the same scenario came up consistently — a pattern, not an isolated incident. Based on real usage and publicly available information. No validation or collaboration from SNCB.

Product
SNCB app (iOS / Android)
Type
Personal concept

The user problem

You're on train IC 123, approaching Brussels-Midi. Your connection — IC 456 to Liège — leaves in 9 minutes from platform 8. You know that. What you don't know:

  • Which platform your current train is arriving on.
  • Which side of the train to exit from (left door or right door) to head in the right direction.
  • Whether platform 8 is a 30-second walk or a 3-minute sprint through a mezzanine level.

The SNCB app today shows the departure platform at your origin station. It doesn't show where you're about to land. So you exit the train, look at a board, pick a direction — and hope.

For a confident traveller this is an annoyance. For someone unfamiliar with the station, running with luggage, or managing a group, it's genuinely stressful — and sometimes, the connection is missed.

Why the data is already there

Infrabel (the Belgian rail infrastructure manager) publishes real-time train position and platform assignment data through its open data API. The SNCB app already uses this data to display live departure times. The arrival platform for a given train at a given station is part of the same dataset — it's just not surfaced to the passenger while they're on board.

My hypothesis

If the journey detail view showed the arrival platform of the current train at the next stop — updated in real time, just like arrival times already are — passengers could position themselves on the train in advance, exit on the correct side, and reach their connection calmly rather than by guessing.

The proposal

Within the existing journey detail screen, add a "Connection" card that appears when a transfer is < 20 minutes away. The card shows:

  • You're arriving on platform 3 — updated live as the train approaches.
  • Next train: IC 456 → Liège, platform 8 — already shown in the app.
  • Exit: right side of train → platform 8 is to your right — derived from platform layout data.
  • A colour indicator for connection comfort: green (plenty of time), orange (tight), red (risky).

No new screen. No new interaction. Just the right information, in the right place, at the right time.

How to measure success

  • Reduction in connection misses on routes where the feature is active (Infrabel operational data).
  • User satisfaction score on the "connections" section of the app (targeted CSAT).
  • Engagement with the connection card (shown vs tapped for detail).

Limits and next steps

Platform layout data (which side leads where) is not always available in a structured format for all Belgian stations — this would need coordination with Infrabel beyond the existing API. Smaller stations with a single platform make the feature trivial; larger hubs (Brussels-Midi, Bruges, Antwerp-Central) are where it matters most.

Next steps: validate the data availability with Infrabel's open data catalogue, prototype the connection card for Brussels-Midi, test with 5 regular commuters who make at least one daily connection.
