Odoo: Three Disconnected Tools, One Coherent Flow

Implementing Sales, Inventory and Accounting for a Belgian SME — and getting the sales team to stop bypassing the stock reservation flow.

Names and data have been anonymised to respect confidentiality.

Role
Functional Analyst, Odoo
Context
Belgian SME, B2B, clients in BE/FR/NL
Duration
~6 months (implementation + hypercare)
Tools
Odoo 17, Jira, Confluence, Excel (migration)

Context

A Belgian company of around twenty employees — selling technical equipment to B2B clients in Belgium, France, and the Netherlands. Before the implementation, three systems were running in parallel: QuickBooks for accounting, a shared Excel workbook for order tracking and stock, and a standalone CRM for customer data. The decision to move to Odoo came from a growth milestone: the volume of manual reconciliation between the three tools had become unsustainable.

Problem

The link between sales and inventory was entirely manual — a salesperson would confirm a quote, mark it in the Excel sheet, and email the warehouse to reserve stock. With no automated check, multiple orders could commit the same references. The warehouse only discovered the conflict at picking time.

Invoicing was a second pain point: Finance generated invoices manually in QuickBooks from a PDF quote, sometimes days after delivery. Discounts negotiated at quote time were not always carried through to the invoice.

Analysis

Fit-gap workshops with sales, operations, and finance. The AS-IS process was a chain of handoffs with no system integration: quote by email, manual Excel update, warehouse email, handwritten delivery note, manual invoice, manual bank reconciliation. Three core gaps emerged.

Gap Analysis

Process step AS-IS Gap Decision
Quote management Word/PDF template sent by email — no version control, no system record No traceability, price revisions untracked, no link to delivery Fit-to-standard: Odoo quotation module — quote confirmed as Sales Order with locked pricing
Stock reservation Manual note in Excel after sales confirmation — warehouse notified by email Multiple orders could commit the same stock; conflict discovered only at picking Fit-to-standard: SO confirmation triggers automatic reservation; unfulfilled demand creates a procurement alert
Pricing transfer Discounts applied at quote time on a PDF — re-entered manually into QuickBooks invoice Frequent discrepancies between quoted and invoiced price; no audit trail Fit-to-standard: invoice generated from the confirmed delivery — pricing locked at SO confirmation, never re-entered
Invoicing timing Invoice created manually in QuickBooks, sometimes 3–5 days after delivery Revenue delay, Finance unable to close the month without chasing Sales Fit-to-standard: invoice triggered automatically on delivery validation
Bank reconciliation Manual matching in QuickBooks — ~1 full day per month Error-prone, time-consuming, no real-time cash visibility Fit-to-standard: Odoo bank sync + auto-matching rules on amount and partner

Pain points: no stock reservation · pricing re-entered manually · invoicing delayed · bank reconciliation = 1 day/month

Proposed solution

  • Standard Odoo O2C flow: Quotation → Sales Order → Automatic stock reservation → Delivery order → Goods issue → Invoice generated → Bank sync.
  • Stock replenishment rules and routes configured for critical references.
  • Bank sync and auto-matching rules by amount and partner.
  • Process documentation and key-user training (sales, warehouse, finance).

Stock conflict surfaced at confirmation, not at picking · pricing locked from quotation · invoice triggered on delivery · bank matched automatically

Two decisions explained

The stock bypass habit. During the first weeks on Odoo, the same pattern from the Excel era resurfaced: sales reps were confirming orders without completing the customer delivery address or reference, creating Sales Orders that couldn't move forward. The instinct was to get the order confirmed quickly and sort out the rest later.

The fix wasn't a technical block. We ran two workshops where the warehouse team showed the sales team what an incomplete order looks like from their side — the calls, the delayed pickings, the pressure at end of month. Once the sales team saw the downstream effect in concrete terms, the behaviour changed. A required-field validation was added as a backstop, not a solution.

The parallel system request. Finance wanted to keep QuickBooks running for three months alongside Odoo — "until we're sure." The proposal was understandable, but running two systems in parallel would have created exactly the same manual synchronisation errors the project was meant to eliminate. With the project lead's support, we agreed instead on a clean cutover with a two-week reconciliation buffer and a named accountant responsible for the transition period. Finance closed the QuickBooks account four months after go-live.

Outcome

Stock shortfalls at delivery were eliminated within the first quarter. Month-end reconciliation dropped from a full day to under two hours. The sales team stopped using the Excel sheet within six weeks. Finance closed the QuickBooks account four months after go-live.

What I took away

The software part was simple. The real work was helping people see that Odoo wasn't a constraint — it was actually solving the constraint they'd already built workarounds for. The sales team wasn't being stubborn; they'd adapted to a process nobody had bothered to design well in the first place. Once that shifted, adoption happened naturally. They didn't choose Odoo because the system was pretty. They chose it because it made their work easier and they could actually see what was happening.
