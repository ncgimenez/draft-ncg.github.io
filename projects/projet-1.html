Case study

O2C Without the Chaos: Standardising SAP SD for a B2B Team

Mapping and fixing a broken order management process during a greenfield SAP S/4HANA Public Cloud implementation.

Names and data have been anonymised to respect confidentiality.

Role
SAP SD Business Analyst
Context
Tech scale-up, hardware e-commerce
Duration
18 months (go-live + rollout)
Tools
SAP S/4HANA Public Cloud, Jira, Confluence, Visio

Context

Greenfield SAP S/4HANA Public Cloud implementation for a Nordic tech scale-up. The B2B sales team managed strategic accounts with custom orders, negotiated discounts and specific delivery timelines. Stakeholders: Sales Operations, Customer Success, Finance, and the Deloitte project team.

Problem

B2B orders were managed entirely outside SAP — through emails and spreadsheets. No Sales Order was created before shipment. Consequences: no credit check, no pricing validation, no order status visibility, invoices sometimes forgotten, and no audit trail in case of dispute.

Analysis

Fit-gap workshops with Sales Operations and Customer Success. AS-IS process mapping: from customer request to delivery. Key gaps identified: no systematic Sales Order, manual credit check (or none), pricing conditions applied from memory.

The B2B team was the most challenging stakeholder: accustomed to email and spreadsheets, they felt that moving into SAP would mean losing the flexibility they relied on for strategic accounts. Addressing that concern was as much a part of the analysis as mapping the process gaps.

Gap Analysis

Process step AS-IS Gap Decision
Order intake Email to Sales Rep — no Sales Order created in SAP before shipment Zero traceability, no credit check, no pricing validation Fit-to-standard: mandatory SO creation in SAP (VA01) before any fulfilment step
Pricing Discounts applied from memory or copied from a personal spreadsheet Inconsistent pricing across accounts, no audit trail in case of dispute Fit-to-standard: pricing conditions configured in SAP (VK11) per customer group and product
Credit check Manual review when remembered, often skipped for known accounts Exposure on overdue B2B accounts — orders shipped despite outstanding balances Fit-to-standard: automatic credit check, orders blocked and released via credit management (FD32)
B2B order approval No approval step — orders processed directly by Sales Rep Large strategic orders shipped without manager sign-off SAP standard approval workflow configured for orders above a defined amount threshold
Delivery Arranged by email with the warehouse team No delivery document in SAP — goods issue not recorded, stock not decremented Fit-to-standard: VL01N delivery order, VL02N goods issue before invoice
Invoicing Manual invoice sent from finance — sometimes forgotten or delayed by weeks Revenue leakage, no dispute trail, Finance unable to reconcile without chasing Sales Fit-to-standard: billing run (VF04) triggered automatically after goods issue

Pain points: no credit check · no pricing validation · invoicing manual and error-prone · zero traceability

Proposed solution

  • Adopt the standard SAP O2C flow: Quotation → Sales Order → Credit check → Delivery → Goods Issue → Invoice → Payment.
  • Configure pricing conditions (B2B discounts by customer and product) in SAP.
  • Set up credit memo and debit memo management for post-billing adjustments.
  • Process documentation and key-user training for Sales Operations and Customer Success.

Every step leaves a trace in SAP · credit and pricing validated automatically · full visibility for all teams

One decision explained

The B2B team's initial position was clear: their accounts were too strategic to go through a standard process. Negotiated pricing, custom delivery timelines, relationships built over years — none of it felt like it could live in a rigid SAP flow.

The decision, backed by the project lead, was to implement standard SAP O2C for all orders with no exceptions — including for B2B strategic accounts. The one adaptation added to address the team's concern: a manager approval step on Sales Orders above a defined amount threshold, configured within SAP's standard order approval workflow. This gave the team the oversight they needed without creating a parallel process outside the system.

In practice, the B2B team still needed time to build confidence in SAP. I personally walked them through their first orders — including returns and credit memo creation — until the flow felt familiar. The resistance didn't disappear on go-live day; it faded order by order.

Outcome

Full traceability for every order in SAP. B2B discounts are systematically validated through pricing conditions rather than from memory. Credit check automatically blocks orders exceeding defined limits. Customer Success can check any order's status in real time without asking Sales.

What I took away

The technical work was maybe 20% of the project. The hard part was the conversation. Sales Operations had built workarounds because the old process didn't work — not because they were stubborn. My job wasn't to convince them SAP was "better." It was to understand exactly what they needed from the old flow, show them SAP could deliver it *plus* the things they couldn't do before (credit checks, pricing rules, visibility), and then walk beside them through the transition until it felt real.

That's what I learned: fit-gap workshops only mean something if you actually challenge the exceptions. Not to reject them, but to understand them deeply enough to find a standard solution that addresses the real need underneath.
