A Planning App That Respects Your Energy

Calendar tools treat every task the same way. This concept rethinks the week around the type of energy each block actually requires.

Personal concept. Not affiliated with any existing product. Inspired by daily use of Apple Calendar, Google Calendar, Sunsama and Fantastical.

The user problem

Every time I look at my week in Apple Calendar or Google Calendar, I see a grid of coloured blocks — but they all feel the same. A 1-hour deep work session looks identical to a 1-hour meeting. A personal appointment sits next to an admin task with no visual distinction.

The result: I can plan a "full" week that's actually exhausting, or a "light" week where I've been avoiding the work that matters. The calendar tells me when, never what kind of week I'm building.

My hypothesis

If a planning app categorised blocks by energy type — deep work, meetings, admin, personal, rest — and showed a weekly balance at a glance, users would make more intentional scheduling decisions and feel less depleted at the end of the week.

The concept

Energy types

Every block is tagged with one of five types, each with its own colour and icon:

  • Focus — deep work, writing, analysis. Needs uninterrupted time.
  • Meetings — calls, syncs, workshops. Social and reactive.
  • Admin — emails, expenses, small tasks. Low-effort but draining if overdone.
  • Personal — gym, errands, appointments.
  • Rest — buffer, lunch, intentional empty space.

Screen 1 — Week view

The week view looks like a standard calendar, but each block is colour-coded by energy type. A thin horizontal bar at the top of the screen shows the week's energy balance — how much of the week is Focus vs Meetings vs Admin. If meetings dominate, the bar turns orange.

[Mockup: week view with colour-coded energy blocks + balance bar at top]

Screen 2 — Adding a block

When creating an event, the app asks for the energy type first — before title or time. It then suggests the best available slot based on the type: Focus blocks in the morning, meetings mid-day, admin at the end of the afternoon.

A "Protect" toggle lets you mark any block as non-negotiable: the app won't suggest filling it and flags any conflict immediately.

[Mockup: new event screen — energy type picker at top, smart slot suggestion below]

Screen 3 — Weekly review (Sunday evening)

A brief review screen compares planned vs actual: which blocks moved, which were skipped, and what the real energy distribution looked like. One question: "What would you protect next week that you didn't this week?"

[Mockup: weekly review — planned vs actual side by side, one reflection prompt]

Why existing apps don't solve this

  • Apple Calendar / Google Calendar — great for scheduling, no concept of energy or intention.
  • Sunsama — close, but heavy and work-focused. No mobile-first design.
  • Motion / Reclaim — AI-driven rescheduling, but takes control away from the user.
  • Fantastical — best-in-class UI, but still time-only, no energy dimension.

The gap: a lightweight, visually calm app (Apple design principles — clarity, deference, depth) that adds intention to scheduling without becoming another productivity system to maintain.

How to measure success

  • Weekly retention rate (do users come back to plan the following week?).
  • Reported stress level at end of week (self-assessment, 1–5 scale).
  • Ratio of Focus blocks protected vs moved (did the app help guard deep work?).

Limits and next steps

This concept assumes users are willing to tag their events — a significant behaviour change. Next step: prototype 3 screens in Figma and test with 5 people who already use a digital calendar daily. Key question: does the energy type picker feel helpful or like extra friction?
