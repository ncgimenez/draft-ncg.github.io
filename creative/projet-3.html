Voma: Planning & Focus Suite

Two apps that work together. One shows you what your week looks like. The other makes sure what you planned actually happens — by putting your tasks where your focus is.

This is a vision for a cohesive system. Both apps exist independently, but they're designed to speak the same language: time as a resource, and focus as something you protect.

Type
Product concept — Planning + Task Management
Components
Voma Calendar (planning) · Voma Focus (execution)
Status
Concept phase — wireframes and user flows in development

The problem Voma solves

Most task managers separate planning from execution. You plan tasks in one place (Todoist, Notion), but when it's time to work, you're in another (your calendar, your timer). The gap between what you said you'd do and what you're actually doing right now is where most focus breaks down.

Voma closes that gap. It's built on a single assumption: you can't focus on a task list, but you can focus on a time block.

Part 1: Voma Calendar — structured planning

A calendar that imposes structure by default. Every event belongs to one of four life categories:

  • Commute — travel time, context switches
  • Work — focused deep work, meetings, collaboration
  • Outside — exercise, errands, time away from desk
  • Personal — reflection, planning, rest

Each category has a default colour. The week becomes legible at a glance — you see the shape of your time, not just a jumble of events.

When you create an event, Voma suggests the category. You can override it, but most of the time the structure just lives there, quietly telling you what kind of week you're building.

One key feature: when you drop a block into your calendar from Voma Focus (see below), the calendar already knows what type of work it is. It colours it correctly. No re-categorizing. No friction.

Part 2: Voma Focus — task execution as time blocks

Voma Focus is where tasks become real. But it doesn't look like a task list.

Instead, it's a canvas. You see:

  • Your calendar (the same one from Voma Calendar) — showing available time
  • A "pending" area where new tasks live
  • A drag-and-drop interface to move tasks into time blocks on your calendar
  • An active timer that runs the current task — with pause, skip, and "done" buttons

The core interaction:

You add a task: "Finish report". It lands in pending. Instead of adding it to a list, you drag it onto your calendar — say, Tuesday 10 AM for 90 minutes. It becomes a calendar block.

When Tuesday 10 AM arrives, Voma Focus pops to the foreground. A timer starts. The task title is huge. Nothing else matters for the next 90 minutes — it's designed to make you forget the task list exists.

When you're done (or when time runs out), you mark it done. The block turns green or gets archived. What's next is already on your calendar.

Key features

Drag-and-drop task placement

No "start time" field. You place a task by dragging it into a time block on your calendar. This makes commitment visible — you see exactly when you're overloading your day.

Real-time adjustments

Running late on a task? Drag it to a new time block. Planned too tight? The calendar shows you the conflict immediately. No surprise overlaps at 4 PM when you realize you double-booked.

Timer that's not punitive

The timer shows time remaining (not elapsed). If you need 5 more minutes, you can extend it with one tap. No judgment, no "failed" notifications. Just: here's the time you asked for, and here's how much is left.

Unified categories

When you drag a task into Focus, you tag it with a category (work, personal, etc.). That same tag flows back to Calendar automatically. No duplicate effort.

Why this matters

Most productivity apps treat time and tasks as separate problems. Voma treats them as the same problem: how do I actually do what I said I'd do?

By making task placement visual and time-bound, Voma removes the abstraction. You're not managing a list. You're defending your time. And that's what focus actually is.

Success metrics

  • Task completion rate (% of tasks placed and completed vs. added and abandoned)
  • Focus quality (time on task without app-switching during a Voma Focus session)
  • Planning accuracy (how often do you need to reschedule vs. staying true to your calendar)
  • Subjective clarity (do users feel they understand their week better?)

Next steps

Design interactive wireframes for both apps. Test the drag-and-drop placement experience with 3–5 users. Interview heavy calendar users to understand what structure they actually want (whether the four-category default is right, or whether it needs adjustment for different lifestyles).

Key question: does seeing your week colour-coded by category actually make you plan differently? And does dropping tasks into time blocks instead of typing them into a list feel like a real change, or just a reskinning of the same idea?
