UX Teardown

Structured Critique of an App

3 friction points identified, 1 detailed solution with hypothesis and success metrics.

"The app is bad" doesn't help anyone. Neither does "here are 47 things I'd change."

A useful critique does three things: it identifies the real friction, it prioritizes ruthlessly, and it goes deep on one fix — not surface-level on many.

The structure: look at the app as a new user. Watch where you hesitate. Where do you have to try twice? Where do you wonder if something is even possible? Write those down. Three of them will capture most of the pain.

Now pick one. Not the most annoying — the one that, if fixed, would make the biggest difference to how someone uses the app. This is your focus.

For that one friction point, go deep: Why does it happen? What's the underlying assumption the designer made that doesn't match how people actually work? What would change if you fixed it? And critically: how would you know the fix worked?

Example: an expense app that makes you classify every receipt immediately. Friction: people don't have categories in mind the moment they capture a receipt — they just want to capture it. Solution hypothesis: capture first, classify later, with a smart default (last-used category). Success metric: time between receipt capture and app close drops by 60%.

That's a critique someone can actually act on. Not because it's kind — but because it's honest about what the problem is and what solving it looks like.
