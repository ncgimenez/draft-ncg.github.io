I Take Things Apart

On the instinct to open things up, understand what's inside, and not be put off by what you find.

I've replaced the battery in my phone myself. That probably sounds like a small thing — and it is — but it requires opening something designed, very deliberately, not to be opened. The screws are hidden. The adhesive is industrial. The instructions online are written by people who assume you've already watched a 12-minute video that assumes you've already done it once before.

Most people don't do it. They hand the phone to a shop or buy a new one. That's a perfectly reasonable choice. But I wanted to understand what was inside before deciding I couldn't touch it.

Logic Pro is the same thing at a different scale. The interface on first launch is genuinely intimidating — mixer channels, routing options, plugin chains, automation lanes, a toolbar that takes months to stop misreading. Every professional tool has a version of this: a surface that says, implicitly, maybe this isn't for you. I'm learning it anyway. Slowly, non-linearly, by doing things wrong and fixing them.

The reason this matters to me professionally is that I do the same thing with processes. When a team tells me "this is just how we do it", I want to know what's under that. Not to change it immediately — but to understand whether "just how we do it" is a deliberate choice, a forgotten constraint, or a workaround that outlived the problem it was solving. Most of the time it's the third one.

The instinct is the same whether I'm prying open a phone or sitting in a fit-gap workshop: look at what's actually there, not what it's supposed to look like. The mess inside a device is honest. The mess inside a process is usually honest too, once you're far enough in for people to stop performing tidiness.

I don't think you need to enjoy disassembly to be good at analysis. But I do think it helps to not be afraid of the moment when something comes apart and you're not sure you can put it back together. That moment is almost always the beginning of understanding what it actually is.
