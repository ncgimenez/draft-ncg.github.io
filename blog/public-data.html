Data Analysis

Public Data → Actionable Recommendation

An open dataset, a structured synthesis, a recommendation for a decision-maker.

There's a gap between "I have this public dataset" and "I know what to do with it."

Most public data projects stop at analysis: here's what I found, here's a chart, here's what the numbers say. Accurate, but unfinished. A decision-maker seeing "the trend is up" still doesn't know whether that means "invest more" or "prepare for saturation."

The work that matters is smaller and more specific: take the dataset, synthesise the patterns, and translate that into a single, actionable recommendation for someone who needs to decide something on Monday morning.

Example: public transit ridership data. The analysis shows ridership is declining in the outer zones while downtown stays stable. So what? The recommendation: "reallocate peak-hour service from outer zones to downtown corridors, and introduce off-peak shuttle service in outer zones. This protects revenue where demand is real, and preserves access where demand is real but differs in timing." Now someone can actually act.

The constraint that makes the work real: the recommendation has to fit inside one email. Not because emails are ideal — because forcing that constraint makes you choose what actually matters. If your recommendation doesn't fit in one paragraph, it's not a recommendation yet. It's still analysis.

The test: a decision-maker reads it and knows whether to say yes, no, or "I need these two things clarified." If they can't tell, the synthesis failed.
