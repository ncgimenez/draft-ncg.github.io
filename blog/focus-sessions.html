Product Concept

Focus Sessions Tied to Your Calendar

A Pomodoro-style timer that knows which life category you're working in — and feeds back into your weekly balance.

The gap between what you plan to do and what you actually do isn't always about discipline. Sometimes it's about visibility.

You have a calendar that tells you you're in a "Work" block, a "Health" block, a "Personal" block. But when you hit a 25-minute focus session, you have no connection between that timer and the life category you're in. You just… sprint and stop.

The idea: a focus timer that reads your calendar. You start a session, and it already knows what category of work you're doing — because it's looking at the calendar event you're currently in. The timer runs; the session ends. Not as a standalone data point, but as a logged session in that calendar event's category.

Over a week, that gives you a real picture: "I planned 3 health sessions and logged 2. I planned 2 personal and logged 0. Work expanded into those blocks." Not as criticism — as data. The kind you can actually act on, because it's tied to decisions you made (or didn't make) about time allocation.

Module 2 of a calendar suite. Module 1 is the calendar itself — the one that structures your week into life categories so you can see what kind of week you're having. Module 2 is the timer that measures whether you're actually protecting those blocks when they matter.

The honest measure: not how many sessions you run, but whether the focused time you logged matches the time you promised yourself.
