Available-to-Promise in Order-to-Cash

Why confirming a delivery date without checking available stock creates cascading problems — and how SAP handles it.

The most expensive broken promise in B2B sales is a delivery date the warehouse can't keep.

Here's the sequence: sales confirms a delivery date. They didn't check stock. Manufacturing is late. Warehouse has to prioritise — but there's already a commitment to the customer. You're now backfilling, expediting, or eating the cost of late delivery. The customer sees unreliability; you see a chaos tax nobody budgeted for.

This is where Available-to-Promise (ATP) comes in. It's not inventory management — it's the promise management layer that sits between sales and what's actually available. When a sales rep enters an order in SAP, they don't see just stock on hand. They see what's confirmed to ship, when it's confirmed to ship, and what's genuinely available for a new delivery date.

The key friction: most teams don't implement it. Sales keeps making commitments without checking, and you keep managing the fallout. The conversations are expensive and the data lives in emails.

When you do implement it, the mechanism is simple. SAP calculates ATP by starting with confirmed stock, subtracting already-committed orders (by delivery date), and showing what's left as available. A sales rep checking availability on October 10 sees: "50 units available for delivery by October 17; 30 units by October 24." They make the commitment with their eyes open.

The benefit isn't just accuracy — it's speed. No more "let me check with warehouse." No more promises made and broken in the same workflow. The sales team moves faster, and the warehouse stops firefighting.
