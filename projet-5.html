SuccessFactors: Why Good Candidates Stop Mid-Application

SAP SuccessFactors is used by thousands of companies to manage recruitment. Its candidate portal reliably drives qualified applicants away before they finish. The friction is avoidable — and I know because I've quit myself.

Personal analysis based on direct experience as a candidate and knowledge of the SAP ecosystem. Not affiliated with SAP or any specific employer using SuccessFactors.

SAP SuccessFactors Recruiting (candidate portal)

The problem — in the first person

I have stopped in the middle of a job application more than once because of SuccessFactors. Not because I changed my mind about the role. Because I was on step 4 of an unlabelled process, had already re-typed my entire work history from scratch, and still had no idea how much further it went.

At some point the calculation shifts: the effort required to finish outweighs the motivation to apply. And I close the tab.

This is not an edge case. A 2023 Greenhouse report found that 60% of candidates abandon applications they started, with process length and repetitive form-filling cited as the top two reasons. SuccessFactors' candidate portal systematically produces both.

[Screenshot: SuccessFactors application form — manual fields for work history, no CV upload parsing]

Why it matters to the business

Every abandoned application is a candidate the company paid to attract (job board, LinkedIn ad, agency) and then lost to its own process. The cost isn't just the lost hire — it's the signal it sends: a painful application experience correlates with a painful onboarding experience. Candidates make inferences.

For high-demand profiles — analysts, engineers, bilingual candidates — the drop-off is steeper. The better the candidate, the more options they have, and the less likely they are to finish a form that treats their time as worthless.

Three friction points in detail

1. The CV exists. The system ignores it.

Most SuccessFactors implementations ask candidates to upload a CV — and then require them to manually re-enter every piece of information from it: dates, job titles, company names, responsibilities. Field by field.

CV parsing technology has been available for over a decade. Tools like Ashby, Greenhouse, and even LinkedIn's own Easy Apply extract structured data from a PDF and pre-fill the form. SuccessFactors supports parsing — but it is not enabled by default, and many implementations never configure it. The result: the candidate does the work the system should do.

2. No progress indicator. No end in sight.

SuccessFactors' standard application flow gives no indication of how many steps remain. There is no progress bar, no "Step 3 of 6", no estimated time to complete. You finish one section and another appears. For a motivated candidate, this is annoying. For a borderline one, it's the moment they close the tab.

Progress indicators are a UX baseline — not a feature. Their absence in a recruitment tool used by major employers in 2025 is a genuine design failure.

3. Sessions expire. Drafts don't save.

Some SuccessFactors configurations time out the session after a period of inactivity. If you stepped away to look up a contract end date, your answers may be gone when you return. There is no automatic draft save. The application starts over.

This is a critical failure for anyone applying from a phone, during a commute, or between meetings — which is to say, most people applying for jobs in 2025.

What a better experience looks like

None of this requires reinventing the product. The gap between SuccessFactors' current candidate experience and a reasonable baseline is three configuration and design decisions:

  • Enable CV parsing by default — pre-fill structured fields from the uploaded document, let candidates correct rather than re-enter.
  • Add a progress indicator — "Step 2 of 4 · about 5 minutes remaining" — visible on every page of the flow.
  • Auto-save drafts — every field change persisted server-side, session resumable from any device.

All three exist in competing ATS platforms. Greenhouse and Ashby ship them out of the box. LinkedIn Easy Apply completes a structured application in under two minutes. SuccessFactors could close this gap without a product rewrite — mostly through default configuration choices and a front-end update to the progress flow.

A note on the SAP angle

I have spent three years implementing SAP and Odoo for operations and finance teams. One of the recurring arguments for SAP is that a unified platform reduces friction: one system, one truth, fewer handoffs. That argument is harder to make when the same platform's candidate-facing product creates friction that drives people away.

The gap between SAP's back-office experience and its front-facing candidate portal isn't a technical constraint — it's a product prioritisation choice. And it has a measurable cost.

How to measure success

  • Application completion rate (started vs. submitted) — target: above 50%.
  • Time-to-complete for a standard application (target: under 10 minutes with CV parsing enabled).
  • Candidate satisfaction score on the application experience (targeted CSAT post-submission).
  • Drop-off rate by step — to identify which screen loses the most candidates.

Limits

This analysis is based on candidate experience and public information about SuccessFactors — not on internal configuration data. Some implementations may have addressed one or more of these issues. The core argument holds: the default configuration, as most candidates encounter it, produces unnecessary friction that drives measurable abandonment.
